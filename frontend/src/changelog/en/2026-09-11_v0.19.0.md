## A Greeting, and a Choice

"Try a demo event" used to drop you straight into a guided tour that had already
started. Nobody had asked for it, and the first thing it did was dim the screen
you had come to look at.

Now the demo opens with a short hello instead: which festival you have landed
in, what your side of it is, and the one thing worth saying out loud — the event
is yours alone, nobody else sees it, and it disappears with the clock at the top
along with everything you got up to in it. Then it asks. *Yes, show me round*
starts the tour; *No thanks, I'll explore* leaves you with a clean, undimmed app
and no tour at all. Changing your mind costs one click: the demo banner offers
the tour for as long as the demo lasts.

## The Tour Starts Where There Is Something to See

The tour's first step used to highlight the page heading, which is a word you
can already read. Both tours now open on something worth pointing at: your next
shift if you are a volunteer, the panel of what needs you if you are organising.
Nine steps and ten, one shorter each than before, because the hello is no longer
one of them.

## Plainer Words Throughout

Every step has been rewritten shorter and more plainly, in both languages. The
talk about none of it being real is gone from the tour entirely — the greeting
and the banner say it once, properly, and the tour gets on with explaining the
product.

## A Second Demo Gets Its Own Start

Trying the volunteer demo and then the organiser one in the same tab used to
carry the first tour across mid-sentence: an organiser being told what they were
already down for as a volunteer, and never greeted at all. The second demo now
starts clean, with its own hello.
