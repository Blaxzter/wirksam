## The Front Page Answers Questions Now

The landing page has a new section at the bottom: **Frequently asked
questions**. What WirkSam costs, whether helpers need an account, who can see an
event, how the reminders reach people, what happens to your data, whether you can
reuse last year's event. These are the things people asked before they signed up,
answered in one place instead of in a reply to each email.

## The German Pages Have Their Own Address

The public pages now exist twice: in English at the usual address and in German
under `/de`. Send a German congregation the `/de` link and they land on German
pages, whatever language their browser is set to. The language switch on those
pages moves you between the two.

## Links Look Like Something

Share a link to WirkSam in WhatsApp, Signal, Slack or an email, and the preview
now shows a proper card: the roster board, the headline and what WirkSam is, in
the language of the page you shared. Before, it showed a cropped screenshot or
nothing at all.

## No More Waiting on a Spinner

The landing page, the changelog and the terms now arrive as finished pages. They
are on screen straight away instead of after a loading spinner, and in the dark
theme they no longer flash white first. The same work makes them readable for
search engines and AI assistants, so somebody asking one about volunteer shift
planning for a church festival has a better chance of hearing about WirkSam.

## Invitation Links Work Before You Have an Account

Opening an invitation link while signed out used to end in "invitation not
found". Now you are offered to sign in or to create an account, and the
invitation comes along: once you are through, you are in the event.

## "Du", Everywhere

The German version had been mixing "du" and "Sie" depending on which screen you
were on. It now says "du" throughout, including the notification emails, which
also lost the last of the old wording like "Taskgruppe". The privacy policy and
the terms stay formal.

## For Anyone Running Their Own WirkSam

Self-hosted installations get a few new settings. Signup can be limited to
invited people, to certain email domains, or to accounts an administrator has
approved. The landing page can be switched off, so the address leads straight to
sign-in, and search engines can be asked to stay away from a private
installation. All of it is described in the deployment guide; with nothing
changed, an installation behaves as before.
