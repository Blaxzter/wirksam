New volunteers tend to ask the same things: where's stand 3, where are the toilets, where should I go. The map answers that, and it shows you where people are still missing while it's at it.

Until you have a map, nothing changes. "Map" only appears in the navigation once a map has a picture or a drawing.

## Upload a picture or draw it yourself

As an organiser you create maps in the event's settings, on the "Map" tab with "Add map". Each map gets a name, then you choose how to start:

- "Draw it yourself" is selected to begin with: an empty area in landscape, portrait or square. The shape can't be changed later.
- "Use a picture" takes a floor plan or a photo as PNG, JPG or WebP, up to 20 MB. Export a PDF as an image first.
- "Start from a template" begins with a drawing you saved earlier.

You can combine the two: you can draw on a picture, and "Put a picture underneath" adds one under a drawing later. The drawing then stretches to the picture's proportions, so check that everything still sits right.

## Drawing the venue

For drawing there's "Rectangle", "Shape", "Line" and "Text". Under "Look" these become a stage, a building, a room, water or a walkway. "Decoration" adds trees, benches, gazebos and beer tables, plus hedges, fences, streams and sand.

A rectangle or shape can be tied to a place under "Linked place". Tapping it then shows that place, and it doesn't need a pin of its own.

Everything saves by itself. "Undo" takes back drawing steps, but not pins you've placed.

## Pins for the places people work

You add a pin for each place people work. "Add pins" lists all the [event's places](/features/shift-planning) that aren't on this map yet. Pick one and tap the spot on the map, or use "Put it in the middle" and drag it to the right spot afterwards.

A place can have one pin per map. You still change its name and details on the "Locations" tab, and replacing the picture leaves the pins where they are.

## Colour shows the staffing

Each place appears on the map as a label with a number, its name and the booked places, for example 3/8. The colour applies to the day shown:

- green, "Fully staffed": every place is booked
- orange, "Partly staffed": at least half are booked
- red, "Understaffed": fewer than half are booked
- grey, "No shifts": there are no shifts there that day

Change the day with the arrows. While the event is running the map starts on today, otherwise on its first day. Beside it, the "All places" list has the same numbers.

Tapping a place shows its description, address and contact plus that day's shifts, and you can take one straight away with "Book". "My shifts" is on and highlights the places where you have a shift that day. "Find a place" searches across all maps.

The counts cover shifts linked to the place. Volunteers see figures for published tasks, and for organisers drafts count too.

Volunteers also come across the map elsewhere: in an open shift under "Location and site plan", and on a task's page when all its shifts share one place. It can go on that task's printout too.

## Symbols for everything else

"Other points" has "Entrance", "Info desk", "First aid", "Toilets", "Parking" and "Kitchen". You can place these symbols as often as you like and give them a "Label", such as "North gate".

They're only for finding your way, with no shifts and no colour. The legend says so too, and the search still finds them.

## Templates

If you run at the same venue every year, you don't have to draw it again. In a map's ⋮ menu, "Save as template…" keeps its drawing and picture, without pins. Areas lose their link to a place, because places belong to the event.

The template is yours: only you can see it, and you can start from it in all your events with "Add map" and "Start from a template". Later changes to the map don't change the template. You can keep up to 20 and rename or delete them in the same list.

## Several maps for one event

An event can have several maps, say "Grounds" and "Hall, ground floor". They sit side by side as tabs, and you change the order in the ⋮ menu with "Move to the left" and "Move to the right".

A place can have a pin on more than one map. Deleting a map removes its pins, and the places themselves stay.

## On a phone

On a phone, the map is under "More". Two fingers zoom and one moves the map, and there are plus and minus buttons too. Tapping a place slides it up from the bottom with that day's shifts and "Book". The search is behind the magnifying glass, the "All places" list and "My shifts" are in the ⋮ menu, and the choice of maps is in the header.

Organisers can work there too. The pencil at the top opens editing, the tools then sit below the map, and "Pins & picture" shows the list of places. You place pins by tapping and move them with your finger.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does a pin stay grey?

Grey means there are no shifts there on the day shown. Check the day, and check the shifts really have this place. A typed-in location doesn't count; it has to be chosen from the list of places. Tasks still in draft only count for organisers.

### Who can see the map?

Everyone in the event, once a map has a picture or a drawing. Only organisers can change it, not people in charge. Nobody outside the event can see it.

### Does the map come along when we copy the event?

Yes. Places, maps, pictures, drawings and pins always come across. The steps are in [Copy an event for next year](/guides/next-year).

### Can volunteers book straight from the map?

Yes. A place shows that day's shifts with "Book". The same rules apply as anywhere else, for example when a shift is reserved for a [team](/features/teams).
